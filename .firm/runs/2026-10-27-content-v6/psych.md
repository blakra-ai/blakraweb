# Trust audit of blakra.us copy

A high-stakes B2B buyer reads carefully, looking for reasons to rule out a small unknown vendor. Their first question is "are they real?" Integrity damage is the hardest trust damage to repair (Kim, Ferrin et al. 2004).

## 1. Five biggest trust risks, ranked

**1. Unchecked things shown as fact.** "BLAKRA Coach · Live", "In pilot", "Four products of our own reach the market", "raising a seed round", "data room", "revenue by line": all still DRAFT. The owner wants sponsors or a buyer, not a round. *Mechanism:* these claims can be tested. A "Live" product with no link, or a deck that never arrives, makes the visitor decide the whole site is invented. *Fix:* show only real products, starting with the skin-analysis AI, at an honest stage: "working prototype · open to a sponsor or buyer". Remove the round and the deck until they exist. *Backfire:* "prototype" reads small, but beats a dead end.

**2. Claims bigger than the company.** "everything your product needs", "We build every layer ourselves", "enterprise AI", "watched day and night", then People shows three principals and per-project hires. *Mechanisms:* words like "everything" and "every" make visitors notice they are being sold to (persuasion knowledge) A firm that claims every category also looks less expert in each (category-spanning penalty: Zuckerman; Leung & Sharkey 2014). Then People contradicts "ourselves". *Fix:* claim responsibility, not headcount: "One team answers for it; specialists join under our lead and contract." Lead with operations systems and AI on an organization's own data. *Backfire:* narrow too far and good-fit buyers leave; keep the full list in Engine.

**3. Proof without a source.** "Five systems that run on their own", "40% fewer stockouts", "20%", "65%": no year, duration, BLAKRA role or measurement method. The cases barely match the owner's own list (admin panels, shops, AI on sensitive data, warehouse systems, bots). They come from the old site, whose other figures the owner dropped. *Mechanism:* a precise number is believed only when its source is visible. If a sales call is vaguer than the site, the gap looks like fabrication. *Fix:* add per case the year, the duration, what BLAKRA built, and "measured by [client system] over [period]". Keep only cases a principal can discuss on a call. *Backfire:* an indefensible source is worse than none; then drop the number, keep the before-and-after.

**4. Few signals that it's a real company.** Email only: no legal entity, no address, abstract avatars instead of faces. "Founded 2024" appears with no history behind it, and the social links have not been checked. *Mechanism:* identity details that are costly to fake act as signals (signalling theory). In the Stanford web-credibility surveys, an address and real people raised trust. That research is older self-report: direction holds, size uncertain. *Fix:* put the entity, jurisdiction and registration number in the footer and on Privacy. Add photos, LinkedIn links and prior experience. Check each social account; an empty one suggests nobody is there. *Backfire:* stock photos are worse than none.

**5. Too many metaphors and mystical words.** "Software with gravity", "last stable orbit", "Map the weight", "every layer beneath it is open", "Crown · Third eye · Sacral", "Past this line, the problem is ours." *Mechanism:* text that is harder to process is judged less true and less clear (processing fluency, Reber & Schwarz). The effect is robust in the lab, smaller in the field, and matches the owner's "unclear". Chakra words also place an engineering firm in a spiritual category. *Fix:* the 3D scene carries the metaphor and the words stay literal. Explain the name once. *Backfire:* strip every image and the brand goes generic, so keep "Software with gravity." as the tagline.

## 2. Section by section

**Hero.** *Works:* a concrete lede and one main button. *Hurts:* the headline doesn't say what BLAKRA does. "Invest in the ecosystem" on the first screen makes a services firm look like a start-up pitching. *Direction:* "We design, build and run software for organizations." Tagline beneath. Investor links to the footer and Products.

**Pulse.** *Works:* the hand-off argument names a pain buyers recognize. *Hurts:* "every layer ourselves", "every one", the orbit note. *Direction:* "One contract, one lead who answers for all of it."

**Proof.** *Works:* the Before/Now contrast. Named stacks show rather than tell; "We keep client names private" explains the gap. *Hurts:* risk 3. The italic "moment" lines are leftovers from the old time-of-day labels. *Direction:* "Five systems in production. Client names withheld under NDA." Card footer: "[year] · [duration] · built by BLAKRA".

**Engine.** *Works:* the Gartner figure is cited with a date. *Hurts:* "one engine every project inherits" is never defined and clashes with "No lock-in". "Open" is chakra language. *Direction:* define the engine or drop it: "Every project starts from our tested base: [modules the owner confirms]. You get all of it as source code."

**Products.** *Works:* "recurring problems become products" is a believable origin. *Hurts:* risk 1. "Making every client build it touches better" is vague. *Direction:* the skin-analysis AI, plus one sentence on what was hard ("written from scratch, without third-party libraries", if exact), then "Open to a sponsor or a buyer."

**Process.** *Works:* the strongest section. Fixed scope and price, code and data ownership, a named engineer, a free assessment and "Not a fit" are costly signals and risk reversal: commitments a client can hold you to. *Hurts:* "Map the weight". "We sit where the work happens" implies on-site work from a remote team. *Direction:* "We walk through the work with the people who do it and name the process that costs most."

**People.** *Works:* "The people you meet are the people who build" is the best trust line: direct accountability. *Hurts:* there are no faces, and "Corporate Compliance, United States" implies a US entity the site never shows. *Direction:* photos, prior experience and LinkedIn for each person. "A principal leads every project; vetted specialists join under our contract and NDA."

**Contact.** *Works:* the specific promised reply (layers, risks, first step) is a free sample (reciprocity); "No sales deck, no mailing list" lowers reactance. *Hurts:* "the problem is ours" promises responsibility before a contract exists. *Direction:* "A principal reads every message." Rename the third door "Sponsor or buy a product".

**Investors.** The core of risk 1. Rebuild as "For sponsors and buyers": what exists, its stage, the ask.

**Privacy.** *Works:* plain and specific. *Fix:* give an actual retention period.

## 3. Rules for the copywriter

**Do**
- State it literally first; a metaphor is optional.
- Test every claim with "how would a buyer check this?"
- Give numbers a source and a date, or use the before-and-after instead.
- Use the owner's own words: admin panel, online shop, warehouse system, bot.
- Say what you don't do, and present the NDA as policy.
- Promise only what you would put in a contract.

**Avoid**
- everything, every, always, genuinely, enterprise, ecosystem, end to end.
- Cosmic or spiritual words in body copy.
- Plans presented as status.
- Wording that implies staff, offices or round-the-clock cover you don't have.
- Clever closing lines. End on a fact.

## 4. Proof the owner should supply, by impact

1. **Identity:** the legal entity, jurisdiction, registration number and address, plus photos and LinkedIn for the three principals.
2. **One real product, shown:** a demo, video or repository of the skin-analysis AI.
3. **Case sources:** year, duration, BLAKRA's role and how each number was measured.
4. **References:** two clients willing to take a call under NDA. Then add "References on request".
5. **Track record:** the principals' work before 2024.
6. **Samples:** redacted screenshots, an architecture diagram, a sample "first read" reply.
7. **Social accounts:** active accounts only.
