# Director's treatment: "An Atlas of Gravity"
The owner asked on 2026-10-28: "We must do better and direct it. The goal is that the site has a special personality and brand, and that its design and story look more special, unique and well made."

## Personality
BLAKRA is the observatory that measured gravity and then built with it. It is precise, calm and warm, and shows its working; it never shouts.

The site is a finely typeset atlas of one black hole, and every specimen in it is alive: real-time 3D objects, labelled the way a scientific plate is labelled. It reads as rigour, so a buyer trusts it, and as art, so people remember it.

**Inference:** agency sites with cosmic themes are dark, neon and full of effects. A paper atlas with live specimens has none of that, and it fits a B2B company whose product is precision.

## Visual language
- **Paper and ink.** Warm paper (#f2f0eb), ink (#0b0b10) and hairlines at 0.75 px.
- **The seven centres as data.** The chakra colours are used only to mean a layer: on the disk, as spectral lines, on the lotuses. They are never decoration.
- **Type.** Each face has one job:
  - Newsreader italic: plate titles and the "human" words.
  - Geist: reading text.
  - Geist Mono, small caps: plate numbers, figure numbers and annotations.
- **The plate.** Each section is "Plate I … VIII". It carries a plate header (number, title, a short epigraph), a figure caption below ("Fig. 1. …"), a reticle of concentric hairline circles with degree ticks around its specimen, and annotations: a label, a hairline leader and a dot on the object.
- **One axis.** A single vertical hairline, the black hole's spin axis, runs through the whole page. Every plate is composed on it.

## Directing rules (motion language)
1. **Match cuts on circles.** Each plate hands over to the next through a circle that becomes the next circle: shadow → spectrograph slit → lotus centre → product ring → orbit → contact orbit.
2. **Gravity easing.** Things accelerate toward a centre and decelerate away from it, with curves derived from orbital speed, not stock easings.
3. **Live annotations.** Leaders draw themselves and stay attached to the moving 3D. Their words are the business message, never filler.
4. **Holds, not hijacks.** A plate holds the screen with CSS sticky while its shot plays; the scroll is always native.
5. **The protagonist.** The visitor's need is a mote in its layers' colours, created in Plate II and followed to the end.
