## Patterns

1. **About = who, where, since when, and why it is shaped this way.** Every firm states a founding year and how it is organised (offices, remote, distributed). The strongest About pages explain a *structural choice*: Pentagram (owners are the primary contact), Planet Argon (small on purpose), 37signals (small teams). Story > stats.
2. **People are named.** Pentagram, Instrument, Netguru and Hashrocket name the leaders. For small firms the names *are* the brand.
3. **Engagement is described as phases and as a relationship, not as a price.** No firm in the set publishes a price or minimum on these pages. thoughtbot frames by product stage; Hashrocket by phase; Planet Argon explains *mechanics* (how billing works, who you talk to, how you see progress) in its FAQ.
4. **FAQ answers the buyer's risk questions.** Where are people, who will I talk to, do you subcontract, how will I know what is happening, what if I have my own developers. Plain, short, specific.
5. **Industries are a short list or a focus.** STX Next names two sectors; thoughtbot four; Planet Argon "many industries… recently…" with seven. Nobody lists 20.
6. **Time zones are stated as a working commitment** ("Teams in your timezone", "available when our clients need us"), not as a map.
7. **Careers is honest even when empty.** thoughtbot: "We currently have no open positions" plus a way to be notified. 10up puts "Apply" next to "Hire us".
8. **Trust without testimonials** comes from: named principals, published method (thoughtbot Playbook, 37signals principles), open source/products (Evil Martians), a security page (STX Next), direct access to the founder (Basecamp's email).

## What wins trust for a small senior firm like BLAKRA

- **Name the three principals and make "you deal with them" the structural point** (the Pentagram move). It is already true in the copy.
- **Publish the terms, not the adjectives**: you own code and data, NDA first, fixed scope and price, working software every two weeks, written support after launch, "we'll tell you if we're not the right fit". These are checkable promises.
- **Explain small-on-purpose** (Planet Argon): principals lead, specialists join under our contract, team scales with the work.
- **Show own products** as proof of build quality (Evil Martians, 37signals). LIOXSA does this.
- **An FAQ that answers procurement's questions** in one sentence each.
- **A direct line**: the founder named, a real email, a 24-hour reply.

## What to avoid

- Big-firm stat blocks (headcount, client counts, NPS, countries) — BLAKRA cannot back them, and the old site's were false. A fake numbers block is the fastest way to lose a careful buyer.
- Logo walls or anonymous testimonials (owner decision: none).
- "VERIFIED" badges, uptime percentages, "ROI guaranteed".
- Long industry lists that read as "anything".
- Dead links (old site: Privacy, Terms, articles, social icons).
- Over-designed About pages that hide the facts a buyer is looking for.
